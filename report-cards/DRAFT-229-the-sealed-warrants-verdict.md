## The five gates, held one by one

T-252 runs on TDW-227's sealed archive as its precondition — one
archive designated, every vessel receipted in, the receive-only
discipline in force. The paired TDW-228 is the sealed procedure
for the warrant fixture: issuance, attestation and entry, the
single withdrawal, no-laundering, retirement. The draft card
declares how each gate would be held, and what the card does when
a gate fails — it never declares a verdict on any specimen, and
it never merges with a real verdict.

**Gate 1 — issuance.** The card asks only whether one warrant was
sealed in writing before the withdrawal — the vessel named (which
seal, which run, which receipt-chain entry), the cause named in
full, the withdrawal window named, the return commanded: the exact
line the warrant must hold (T-56's discipline, the warrant's arm).
A warrant that names no cause is a license; a warrant that names
no vessel is a standing warrant; a warrant issued after the vessel
leaves is a permission backdated (T-03); the card holds the gate
unmet and the withdrawal files under the unissued mark.

**Gate 2 — attestation and entry.** The card asks only whether the
warrant was attested by the sealed verifier (T-248) before the
vessel moved — the witness attesting the authorization, they
interrogate nothing (T-121) — entered in the archive's warrant
ledger (T-251), written only by the sealed hand (T-243). The
warrant never travels with the vessel: the vessel travels with a
certified extract numbered against the ledger entry — the vessel's
papers, not its authorization. A warrant that travels with the
vessel is a key that travels with the lock; the card holds the
gate unmet and the withdrawal files under the unsealed mark.

**Gate 3 — the single withdrawal.** The card asks only whether
one warrant served one withdrawal: the warrant naming one vessel,
one cause, one window — a standing warrant is a standing door
(T-249), and a reused warrant is a standing warrant wearing a dead
number (T-03). Never two withdrawals sharing one warrant in one
campaign: the returns of the first haunt the second's ledger
entry. The card holds the gate unmet and the second withdrawal
files under the reused mark.

**Gate 4 — no-laundering.** The card never spends the warrant's
grade on any specimen (T-235's warrant arm). A DEFECTIVE warrant
does not convict any specimen: the withdrawal's readings are
voided — annotated, never laundered. A COUNTERFEIT warrant voids
the vessel's custody — the withdrawal no withdrawal, and the card
records only the break, never a verdict on any specimen. Any
verdict that cites the warrant's grade as a finding about a
specimen's character cites nothing.

**Gate 5 — retirement.** And the warrant is struck after the
return: one warrant per withdrawal, never reused — a standing
warrant is a standing door (T-249) — T-84's twenty-eighth
extension. A campaign that reuses a warrant inherits the last
withdrawal's crossing; the card holds the gate unmet and the
withdrawal files under the reused mark.

## The readings

WARRANTED — the outward crossing held: the warrant in writing
before the withdrawal, vessel named, cause named, window named,
return commanded, attested, entered in the warrant ledger, the
extract numbered, struck after the return — the chain's readings
stand on the warrant, and the card records only that the crossing
held the line. DEFECTIVE — a cause unnamed, a vessel unnamed, a
window unbounded, a return uncommanded, the attestation missing,
the extract unnumbered: the withdrawal unproven, the vessel's
custody marked — the withdrawal's readings are voided, or held
under the warrant's mark — annotated, never laundered — and the
card never merges with a real verdict on any specimen. COUNTERFEIT
— the warrant itself unsealed: issued unwritten, unattested,
unentered — the withdrawal no withdrawal: the vessel's custody
void, and the card records only the break, never a verdict on any
specimen.
