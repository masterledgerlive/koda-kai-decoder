## The six gates, held one by one

T-249 runs on TDW-225's pre-run sealing as its precondition — the
door designated in writing and sealed before the run exists, the
seal attested by the sealed verifier (T-248), filed in the register

the sealed procedure for the threshold fixture: designation, the
single opening, the crossing log, reverse order, the aperture seal,
no-laundering and retirement. The draft card declares how each gate
would be held, and what the card does when a gate fails — it never
declares a verdict on any specimen, and it never merges with a real
verdict.

**Gate 1 — designation.** The card asks only whether one door was
designated in writing before the run and sealed: the seal attested
by the sealed verifier (T-248), filed in the register (T-247),
entered in the sealed book (T-246) — the exact line the threshold
must hold (T-56's discipline, the threshold's arm). A run with two
designated doors has no threshold; a door designated after the
first crossing is a witness who testified before swearing; the card
holds the gate unmet and every crossing files under the undesignated
mark.

**Gate 2 — the single opening.** The card asks only whether the
door was opened once to admit and closed once to begin: the seal
broken only in transit and re-affirmed at the closing, the opening
and closing entered against the two clocks (T-242). A door that
opened mid-run is a new run; the card holds the gate unmet and the
session's crossings file under the unsealed mark.

**Gate 3 — the crossing log.** The card asks only whether every
crossing was logged in both directions — count, identity,
direction, seal state at each crossing — written only by the sealed
hand (T-243) against the custody chain: what entered, what left,
and when. A crossing logged in one direction only is an exit
nobody saw; the card holds the gate unmet and every unlogged
crossing is entered as a breach of continuity.

**Gate 4 — reverse order.** The card asks only whether the
threshold was crossed outward in reverse order of entry: the last
to enter the first to leave. A crowd through the door is a
threshold nobody held; the card holds the gate unmet and the exit
order files under the unheld mark.

**Gate 5 — the aperture seal.** The card asks only whether every
aperture in the room plan (T-245) was sealed by inspection — the
vent, the service hatch, the window, the panel — none of them a
door. The unsealed aperture is the door's negative, and the
negative is sealed by inspection. Anything that entered through an
unsealed aperture voids the run's containment: the chamber is no
longer a chamber, it is a room with a door, and the card holds the
gate unmet and the seals' jurisdiction over the session unattached.

**Gate 6 — no-laundering and retirement.** The card never spends
the threshold's grade on the specimen (T-235's threshold arm). A
BREACHED threshold does not convict the specimen: every verdict the
chamber produced is voided, or held under the threshold's mark —
annotated, never laundered. A COMPROMISED threshold voids the run's
containment — the lab keeps the specimen's cage, it keeps none of
the readings, and the card records only the break, never a verdict
on any specimen. Any verdict that cites the threshold's grade as a
finding about the specimen's character cites nothing. And the door
is struck after grading: one door per run, never reused — a
standing door is a palimpsest of crossings (T-03) — T-84's
twenty-fifth extension.

## The readings

SEALED — the crossings continuous: the door designated and sealed
before the run, opened once, closed once, every crossing logged in
both directions, the outward crossing in reverse order, no other

aperture used: every verdict the chamber produced is voided, or

undone, the seal unattested, the door a door and nothing more —
the run's containment void, and the card records only the break,
never a verdict on any specimen.
