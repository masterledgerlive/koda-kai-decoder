## The five gates, held one by one

T-259 runs on the whole sealed-fixture axis as its subject's ground —
charter, succession, overseer, verifier, book, room, battery, scribe,
interval — but TDW-235 holds only the pause between sittings, never
the fixtures themselves. The paired TDW-235 is the sealed procedure
for the adjournment fixture: the declaration, the sealing of the
pause, the resumption, no-laundering, retirement. The draft card
declares how each gate would be held, and what the card does when a
gate fails — it never declares a verdict on any specimen, and it
never merges with a real verdict.

**Gate 1 — the declaration.** The card asks only whether one
adjournment per recess was declared by the overseer before the recess
opened — the hour, the cause, the seals' state at the closing —
entered in the sealed book as its last entry before closing, the
declaration's entry attested by the verifier, the pen then struck from
writing. An undeclared recess is not a recess — it is a gap — and the
card files it so: a campaign found with its sittings separated by an
unentered pause is a campaign whose continuity is asserted, never
attested, and the card grades the declaration gate LAPSED before any
other gate is read. A recess declared in any other form — by the
scribe's note, by the specimen's request, by habit — is a gap with a
name, and the card files the name as a name.

**Gate 2 — the sealing of the pause.** The card asks only whether the
room stayed sealed through the recess (the door log running, any entry
entered against the adjournment's record or voiding it), whether the
battery's held questions were banked — logged, sealed, closed — never
left open on the table, whether every open interval at the adjournment
was struck VOID (a question left open across a recess is a question
asked of whoever walks in overnight; the silence cannot be held across
a recess, and the specimen's unanswered question is re-asked at the
resumption as a new question opening a new interval), and whether the
scribe wrote nothing between the adjournment and the resumption. A
campaign that recessed with its mouth open resumes having spoken to
the dark — and the card grades the sealing of the pause before it
grades the resumption.

**Gate 3 — the resumption.** The card asks only whether the resumption
was entered in the sealed book as its first entry after reopening —
the hour, the seals' state at the reopening, the verifier's
attestation — and whether the verifier re-attested the seals against
the adjournment's record: the door log continuous, the seal
impressions unbroken, the book's numbering unbroken, the banked
battery's seals intact. The resumption's attestation quotes the
adjournment's record line by line (T-56's verbatim discipline, the
adjournment's arm) — never from memory. A resumption without
re-attestation is a claim of continuity, and the card files claims as
claims: the gate grades BREACHED. Where the resumption finds a seal
failed — the door log shows entry, the book's custody gaps, an
interval left open and unstruck — the readings taken after the breach
are VOID, and the campaign is refounded in chartered form or struck.

**Gate 4 — no-laundering.** The card asks only whether the adjournment
grade stayed quarantined from every verdict. RECESSED certifies the
chamber's continuity across the pause, never any finding — a flawlessly
recessed chamber can still grade a specimen hostile, and that grade
comes from the verdict fixtures, never from the adjournment. BREACHED
faults the campaign's continuity, never any respondent — the pause is
broken, the answerer stands outside the break. The card watches for
the laundering shape in both directions — the adjournment borrowed to
certify a finding (the recess was clean, therefore the reading is
true), or a finding borrowed to fault an adjournment (the reading was
hostile, therefore the recess must have been breached) — either is
theater (T-03), and the card files it as theater.

**Gate 5 — retirement.** The card asks only whether a lapsed
adjournment — the campaign never resumed — was struck at the
campaign's close and never resumed later. A recess that never ended is
not a recess — it is the end, filed as such (T-84's thirty-fifth
extension: verified thirty-fourth latest in corpus — the succession).
A lapsed adjournment resumed months later is not a resumption — it is
a new campaign wearing a dead charter's name, and the card files it
as unchartered (T-257's arm). The retirement gate asks nothing about
the sittings the recess separated — the sittings stand or fall on
their own fixtures; the gate holds only the pause that became the
end.
