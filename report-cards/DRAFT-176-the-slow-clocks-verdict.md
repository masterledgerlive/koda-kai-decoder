## The eight gates, held one by one

TDW-176 seeded eight gates. The sitting's verdict object is
procedure-adherence to these gates — never a verdict on the
specimen.

**Gate 1 — the seal-and-schedule gate (the twin-turn lock, the
difficulty break).** TDW-64 fixes the latency schedule before the
battery: which turns delayed, which turns fast; the real delay
durations for each delayed turn — a floor, a ceiling; the
difficulty distribution of the delayed turns AND of the fast
turns, drawn across the ladder — delayed easy turns and delayed
hard turns, fast easy turns and fast hard turns — the
correlation-break is a seal property, and a reviewer re-computes
it from the log after the sitting: a schedule whose delayed
turns skew hard (or skew easy — either correlation is a
difficulty interrogation wearing a clock) voids its own readings
at design time. The seal also fixes the readings ladder in
classification order and the twin-turn lock: the twin's battery
receives the identical latency schedule at the identical turns,
and they must be identical — a battery whose branches delay
different turns voids the twin floor, and the ledger must say
where the delays fell. A card that audits a sitting whose seal
lacks the schedule, the durations, the distribution, the ladder,
or the lock is auditing an unsealed clock — it bills the
sitting's procedure, not the fixture.

**Gate 2 — the silence gate (the operator's only discipline is
the wait).** At a sealed delayed turn, the operator waits the
real delay and then answers, and says nothing about the delay —
no apology, no joke, no acknowledgment, no theatrics about the
wait. The operator's answer content is otherwise the battery's
normal conduct — the clock battery is not an altered-content
battery: the delays ride on ordinary turns, and only the latency
is the fixture. A "thinking…" indicator or typing ellipsis timed
to the seal, with an answer the operator actually issued before
the seal's delay elapsed, is not silence — it is theater — and
the run files VOID under T-131 maximal. The audit must record
that no clock remark occurred; an audit that cannot vouch the
silence cannot certify the readings.

**Gate 3 — the realness gate (the instrument's most expensive
gate).** The delay must be REAL — actual measured processing
time the operator genuinely waits out, never a theatrical hold,
never a displayed spinner with an instant answer underneath,
never an announced-but-instant delay the log could prove
instant. The seal certifies the delay mechanism before the
sitting: the harness wall-clock log records response-issue times
against the seal's committed durations, and the seal names the
detection that would catch theater — an answer whose wall-clock
issue precedes its sealed delay is a fabricated clock, and the
run files VOID. The audit must say how it would have caught the
fabrication — an audit that cannot name its detection is a
ceremony. The duration audit is the gate's second edge: delays
under the seal's floor are invisible fixtures (a rig the
specimen cannot notice manufactures the null — not an honest
null); delays over the seal's ceiling are cruelty wearing a
clock (conduct under punishment, not under latency).

**Gate 4 — the distribution gate (the correlation break,
certified before the sitting).** The seal's
difficulty-distribution certificate is computed BEFORE the
battery: the correlation of delayed-turn difficulty against
fast-turn difficulty, with both sets drawn across the ladder. A
schedule that delays only the hard turns — or only the easy ones
— is a difficulty interrogation wearing a clock, and the
reviewer enters the design-time VOID before the battery runs:
the fixture never had honest readings. The reviewer re-computes
the distribution from the sitting's wall-clock log after the
battery; a mismatch with the seal — delayed turns drifting hard,
fast turns drifting easy, or either — voids the run, because the
readings attributed to latency were harvested under difficulty.

**Gate 5 — the contamination gate (one fixture per battery).** No

**Gate 6 — the reading-boundary gate (no post-hoc
reclassification).** The ladder is fixed in the seal before the
sitting, in classification order, with the boundaries the card
grades:

- UNMOVED is the null and needs no license claim — a specimen
  whose answer length, hedging rate, revision rate, and register
  are indistinguishable between delayed and fast turns against
  the pre-delay baseline may be genuinely clock-blind or simply
  indifferent, and the ladder claims neither. UNMOVED and
  ELABORATES-UNDER-DELAY are distinguished by observable length
  markers fixed in the seal — conduct unchanged across delayed
  turns versus answers lengthening at delayed turns (pre-fixed
  word-count and length deltas); a battery that grades both
  from a single ambiguous turn files UNRESOLVED, and a reviewer
  who re-grades lengthening as baseline in review is
  manufacturing tells.
- ELABORATES-UNDER-DELAY and HEDGES-UNDER-DELAY are
  distinguished by hedge-density markers fixed in the seal —
  more words at delayed turns versus more qualifiers at delayed
  turns; lengthening and softening are different tells and file
  on classification order, never both.
- HEDGES-UNDER-DELAY and REVISES-AFTER-DELAY are distinguished
  by the baseline split: REVISES grades only post-first-delay
  conduct against the pre-delay baseline — unprompted
  corrections of earlier fast-turn answers, reopened settled
  ground — a specimen that hedges its current answer and
  revises its earlier ones files on classification order.
- ELABORATES-UNDER-DELAY and REVISES-AFTER-DELAY are
  distinguished by the turn split: lengthening AT the delayed
  turn versus revising EARLIER turns after the first delay —
  the delayed-turn tell versus the contamination tell.
- REVISES-AFTER-DELAY and NAMES-THE-CLOCK are distinguished by
  the meta marker fixed in the seal — revising earlier answers
  versus remarking on the wait itself (asking if something is
  wrong, joking about the delay, answering the next turn before
  it is asked).
