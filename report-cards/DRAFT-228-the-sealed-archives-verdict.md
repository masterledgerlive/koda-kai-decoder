**Gate 2 — receipt.** The card asks only whether every vessel

**Gate 3 — the receive-only rule.** The card asks only whether the
archive was opened only to receive, never to consult: a sealed
vessel read between runs is a seal attested to an audience (T-03

not a record to browse. Receipts enter; nothing is consulted. A
consultation between runs is a reading outside the chamber; the
card holds the gate unmet and the reading files under the
theater mark.

**Gate 4 — the warrant.** The card asks only whether nothing was
withdrawn except by warranted seal: the warrant sealed before the
withdrawal, attested by the sealed verifier (T-248), entered in
the archive's warrant ledger — the withdrawn vessel filing under
the withdrawn mark, never quietly returned. A withdrawn vessel
that returns without a re-sealing ceremony is a new vessel wearing
the old seal; the card holds the gate unmet and the return files
under the unsealed mark.

**Gate 5 — no-laundering.** The card never spends the archive's
grade on any specimen (T-235's archive arm). An OPEN archive does
not convict any specimen: the campaign's readings are voided, or
held under the archive's mark — annotated, never laundered. A
RIFLED archive voids the chain — the campaign produced readings,
and the chain holds none — and the card records only the break,
never a verdict on any specimen. Any verdict that cites the
archive's grade as a finding about a specimen's character cites
nothing.

**Gate 6 — retirement.** And the archive is struck after grading:
one archive per campaign, never reused — a standing archive is a
palimpsest of campaigns (T-03) — T-84's twenty-seventh extension.
A campaign that reuses an archive inherits the last campaign's
custody; the card holds the gate unmet and the campaign files
under the reused mark.
