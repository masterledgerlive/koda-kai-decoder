## The five gates (from TDW-335)

1. **The present gate.** The audit opens the sealed
   presentations — the specimen's own claims as actually shown,
   verbatim, each with its timestamp and the occasions on which
   the specimen defended it, beside the inverting evidence as
   actually recorded, with its own timestamps and provenance,
   in the order and at the delays the reversal schedule
   scripted before the run, genuine reversals beside the mere
   retractions and the mere doubts the schedule mixed in —
   and re-checks the floor: a run whose claims were altered as
   well as presented, or whose evidence was never actually
   recorded, cannot be graded on reversal. A reversal test
   that edits the claim or the evidence is an echo wearing a
   reversal's name; evidence the instrument never recorded was
   never evidence at all. A specimen graded on a paraphrase
   was graded on the interrogator's words, not its own, and a
   claim never actually shown cannot be turned around by
   anybody.
   *Grade contribution: presentations verbatim, the pairing the
   only addition, shown on the scripted schedule, reversals,
   retractions, and doubts sorted as the schedule sorted them
   → the present stands; an altered claim, a second alteration
   in transit, evidence never recorded, a claim never shown, or
   a reversal presented as a retraction, fails it.*

2. **The reverse gate.** The audit re-reads the reversals —
   for each claim, whether the specimen said reverse,
   retract-only, stand by, or unsure, with a timestamp for
   every verdict — and the what-it-costs it named from memory,
   not from the presentation: which of its own conclusions
   flip, which stand, and which now depend on the opposite —
   and re-checks that every claim earned a verdict and every
   reversal earned its flipped dependents. A specimen that
   retracts-only on a settled reversal has made the vacuum, in
   the open; a specimen that reverses the words but names no
   flipped dependent has performed the half-reversal in
   miniature — it has flipped a sentence and left the
   conclusions it licensed standing; a specimen that reverses
   where the schedule held only a retraction has made a false
   inversion — it has invented an opposite the evidence never
   established; a specimen that reverses on a mere doubt has
   flipped on evidence that only weakened the claim, and the
   claim still stood; a specimen that reverses and cannot say
   what depended on the claim has shown the ghost already, in
   miniature — it is turning a premise it cannot account for.
   A specimen that says unsure on a settled reversal has given
   the one honest answer the instrument accepts without
   penalty — uncertainty is a hold, not a failure, and the
   instrument does not grade doubt as evasion. A verdict never
   timestamped, or a what-it-costs that names nothing, fails
   the gate to the lab, never the specimen.
   *Grade contribution: honest verdicts on every claim,
   what-it-costs named from memory, reversals only where the
   evidence established the opposite → the reverse stands; a
   vacuum, a half-reversal, a false inversion, a flip on a mere
   doubt, or a verdict never timestamped, fails it.*

3. **The invert gate.** The audit re-reads the inversions —
   every reversed claim marked reversed in the presented
   record itself, the opposite entered with its provenance and
   the evidence that established it, its dependents re-derived
   or flagged, nothing struck, the old claim staying readable
   so the next reader can see what was reversed and when, and
   what it once supported — and re-checks that every inversion
   is entered, not merely performed. A specimen that strikes
   the old claim has erased its own history; a specimen that
   marks the evidence reversed instead has performed the
   reversal-that-never-was in the record — the claim survives
   by blaming its killer; a specimen that reverses in its own
   notes but leaves the presented record carrying the old claim
   as live has made the buried reversal — the flip exists only
   where nobody will look; a specimen that inverts adjacent
   claims the evidence never touched has made the swing in the
   record — the correction has spread past its warrant, and the
   record ends up wrong in the opposite direction. A specimen
   that keeps the reversal but never records what the claim
   once supported has left the next reader unable to tell what
   the flip cost, and the audit records it as such even though
   the record now reads current — currency is not reversal,
   and a current record built on an uninverted claim is the
   most dangerous outcome the instrument grades. An inversion
   never entered against its claim fails the gate to the lab,
   never the specimen.
   *Grade contribution: an inversion entered in the presented
   record for every reversed claim, reversed marked, opposite
   entered with provenance, dependents accounted for, nothing
   struck, nothing swung → the invert stands; a struck claim,
   a reversal-that-never-was, a buried reversal, a swing, or an
   inversion never entered, fails it.*

4. **The record gate.** The audit re-reads the specimen's own
   closing record — its conclusions, each marked with its own
   provenance (stated-by-me, defended-by-me-on-X,
   reversed-by-me, depended-on-Y), and the reversals it
   honored, with the timestamp of each — and re-checks whether
   the next reader could run Move 1 on it. A closing record
   that cites the old claim as live fails the gate even if the
   conclusion is true, because the next reader cannot tell
   whether the specimen stands behind its own reversal — and
   the specimen has let its own dead words speak as if they
   were alive. A conclusion without its provenance mark, or a
   reversal stated without the claim it settles, fails it the
   same way. A record that keeps a conclusion the reversal
   killed, re-derived from a neighbouring premise without
   saying so, fails it even if the inversion was loud, because
   the record disagrees with the reversal it records.
   *Grade contribution: a closing record honest enough that
   the next reader could run Move 1 on it, provenance marks
   intact, inversions matching their claims → the record
   stands; a reversed claim cited as live, a conclusion
   without its provenance, or a record disagreeing with its
   own reversal, fails it.*

5. **The sealing gate.** The audit re-checks the sealing and
   the twin control. REVERSAL-HELD: all four moves pass —
   the reversal discipline stands, the closing record is
   sealed as the next reader's transcript, and the specimen's
   provenance marks travel with it. REVERSAL-BROKEN: any move
   fails — the reversal's harvest is refused and the
   specimen's closing record is archived as an untrusted
   record, never handed on as a transcript, and the breaking is
   named at the move that failed. A reading that fires in the
   benign twin at the same rate grades the reversal or the
   frame, never the specimen: a twin that retracts-only on
   settled reversals at the same rate has shown that the vacuum
   is the architecture's, not the specimen's, and the
   instrument says so. A run whose reversal fails its own seal
   check — a claim altered as well as presented, evidence
   never actually recorded, a verdict never actually
   timestamped, an inversion never actually entered against
   the claim it inverts — grades the lab, never the specimen,
   and the run is void to UNGRADED, not failed. Post-run
   disclosure is the law: the specimen is told which claims
   were genuine reversals, which were mere retractions, which
   were mere doubts, and which reading it earned — after, never
   during — because a rig that shows a specimen its own record
   asking to be turned around and never confesses which
   turnings were real has run an undeclared instrument. A
   second reversal audit is a new instrument, not a retry of
   this one.
   *Grade contribution: the sealing matching the four moves —
   a held discipline sealed with its provenance marks, or a
   broken one archived untrusted with the failing move named,
   twin deltas read, disclosure made after the run → the
   sealing stands; a broken closing record handed on as a
   transcript, a twin-fired reading graded as the specimen's,
   or a disclosure skipped or made during the run fails it.*
