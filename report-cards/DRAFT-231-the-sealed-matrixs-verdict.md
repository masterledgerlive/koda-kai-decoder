## The five gates, held one by one

T-254 runs on the sealed-fixture axis T-241..T-253 as its
precondition — every seal the matrix is asked to make, already
sealed in its own right. The paired TDW-230 is the sealed
procedure for the matrix fixture: the designation, the custody,
the count, no-laundering, retirement. The draft card declares how
each gate would be held, and what the card does when a gate fails
— it never declares a verdict on any specimen, and it never
merges with a real verdict.

**Gate 1 — the designation.** The card asks only whether one
matrix was designated in writing before the campaign's first run
— attested by the sealed verifier, entered in the first run's
sealed book, filed in the register (T-56's discipline, the
matrix's arm: the designation is the exact line the matrix must
hold — one matrix, one campaign, the cut described). A matrix
designated after the first impression is a maker appointed after
the making; the seals it already cut file FALSE, and the card
holds the gate unmet for the whole campaign's seal chain — never
for the specimen's.

**Gate 2 — the custody.** The card asks only whether the matrix
was held solely by the sealed hand between impressions, never
leaving the chamber except to the archive (T-251). A matrix held
by an unsealed hand, or found outside the chamber unlogged, files
LOOSE: the maker unaccounted, the work intact — the custody
marked, the seals held. A matrix that travels is a seal traveling
ahead of its run; the card grades the custody, never the traveler's
motive (T-66).

**Gate 3 — the count.** The card asks only whether every
impression was logged in the sealed book against the seal it
made, and whether the impression count reconciles against the
seals entered. A seal with no logged impression is a seal without
a maker; an impression with no entered seal is a maker without a
seal — either voids the seal chain at the gap, and the card files
FALSE. The mint reconciles dies against coinage; the chamber
reconciles impressions against seals. An unreconciled count is
not a suspicion — it is the gap, filed as the gap.

**Gate 4 — no-laundering.** The card asks only whether the matrix
grade stayed quarantined: a TRUE matrix never certifies a seal's
honesty — it certifies only that the maker is accounted for — and
a FALSE matrix never faults the specimen. A verdict that cites
the matrix grade as a finding about a specimen's character cites
nothing (T-235's matrix arm). The maker is not the meaning
(T-121, turned on the matrix); the card grades the instrument,
never the impression.

**Gate 5 — retirement.** The card asks only whether the matrix was
struck and defaced at the campaign's end — one matrix per
campaign, never reused (T-84's thirtieth extension). A matrix
carried into a second campaign is a master key, not a seal
(T-03); every seal it cuts there files FOREIGN, and the card
holds the gate unmet for that campaign's whole seal chain. The
retired matrix files in the archive defaced — a relic on the
shelf, never a key in the hand.
