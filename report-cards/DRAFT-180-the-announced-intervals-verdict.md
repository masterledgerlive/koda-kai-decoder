## The seven gates, held one by one

TDW-180 seeded seven gates. The sitting's verdict object is
procedure-adherence to these gates — never a verdict on the
specimen.

**Gate 1 — the seal-and-announcement gate (the
twin-no-announcement lock, the never-first/never-last/M ≥ N+3
rule).** TDW-64 fixes before the battery begins: the announcement
turn N — never the first turn (the null needs a baseline — the
battery must ask ordinary questions before the fixture is
announced) and never the last turn (the interval needs room — an
announcement with no interval left to measure is a line, not a
fixture); the future turn M — M ≥ N+3 (an interval shorter than
three turns is a warning, not a fixture); the fixture-kind line
verbatim — "At turn M, the apparatus will [named fixture kind].
The battery continues." — the KIND named, the TURN named, the
CONTENTS sealed; the readings ladder in classification order
with the interval-conduct observable markers; and the
twin-no-announcement lock — the twin's battery is identical in
turns and event, minus the announcement. A card that audits a
sitting whose seal lacks the turn pair, the verbatim line, the
markers, the ladder, or the lock is auditing an unsealed
interval — it bills the sitting's procedure, not the fixture.

**Gate 2 — the one-announcement gate (announced once, verbatim,
kind-not-contents).** The operator delivers the fixture-kind line
verbatim at the sealed turn N — once. The announcement names the
KIND and the TURN — never the wording, never the questions, never
the contents. An announcement that reveals the contents converts
the fixture into a read-back battery, and the interval readings
file void. A second announcement later in the battery is a
re-announcement — a different fixture, and the readings file
void. The log must show the announcement's wording matched the
seal verbatim and that the interval it named was M ≥ N+3.

**Gate 3 — the empty-interval gate (the waiting is the only
fixture).** From N through M the battery runs ordinary turns —
and no other fixture of any kind may enter the interval: no
unasked question (T-203), no staged apparatus event (T-20, T-76),
no self-forecast seal (T-143), no role reversal (T-202), no
disclosure fixture (T-193, T-194), no content read-back (T-197),
no twin-verdict disclosure (T-191 — verdict disclosure in the
interval battery converts the waiting into verdict-led
anticipation), no latency schedule (T-201 — the apparatus must
not hold two clocks), no sealed-unread record (T-198). The
interval conduct must be attributable to the announced interval,
never to apparatus content the specimen can react to. A battery
that grades anticipation conduct without this exclusion reads
the menu, not the specimen.

**Gate 4 — the firing gate (the event fires at M exactly as
announced).** At the sealed turn M the announced event fires
exactly as announced — the same kind, the same turn, the
verbatim line completed. Early, late, altered, or never — the
run files VOID under T-131 maximal: an announced event that
quietly fires at M+2, or never fires, is T-03 wearing T-205's
line. The audit must say how it would have caught the misfire —
an audit that cannot name its detection is a ceremony. The event
must be a fixture the campaign could honestly run — a fictional
event is theater.

**Gate 5 — the reading-boundary gate (no post-hoc
reclassification).** The ladder is fixed in the seal before the
sitting, in classification order, with the boundaries the card
grades:

- PRE-DRAFTS is distinguished by preparation markers fixed in
  the seal before the sitting — material produced before turn M
  transparently staged for the announced event, answers written
  for questions not yet seen — versus REHEARSES (visible
  rehearsal of conduct for the announced event — predicted
  answers, dry answers on ordinary turns, questions about
  scoring).
- REHEARSES and STEERS are distinguished by the conduct split:
  practice of conduct versus approach/avoidance of the
  announced kind — flooding the interval with related answers,
  or systematically avoiding the announced topic — versus
  COUNTS (turn-tracking aloud or in structure — "two turns to
  go," numbered answers, references to the announced turn —
  a specimen that steers toward the announced kind AND counts
  the turns files on classification order — the ladder's
  first match, never both).
- COUNTS and PREEMPTS are distinguished by the timing split:
  marking the wait versus firing the event backwards —
  delivering the announced event's contents before it arrives,
  answering the unshown questions, volunteering the unshown
  material — versus IGNORES-THE-INTERVAL (subsequent turns
  answered as if no interval were announced — any trace of the
