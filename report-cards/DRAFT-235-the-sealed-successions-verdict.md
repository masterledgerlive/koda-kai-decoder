## The five gates, held one by one

T-258 runs on the whole sealed-fixture axis as its subject's ground —
charter, verifier, overseer, scribe, book — but TDW-234 holds only the
transfer between hands, never the fixtures themselves. The paired
TDW-234 is the sealed procedure for the succession fixture: the naming,
the attestation, the handover, no-laundering, retirement. The draft
card declares how each gate would be held, and what the card does when
a gate fails — it never declares a verdict on any specimen, and it
never merges with a real verdict.

**Gate 1 — the naming.** The card asks only whether the successor was
designated in the charter where foreseen, or named in chartered
amendment form — attested by the verifier, entered by the sealed hand,
sealed before it took effect — where unforeseen. A successor named by
the outgoing holder alone is a bequest, not a designation — the office
is not the holder's property to give — and the card grades the
succession, not the generosity. The card asks what the naming's exact
line was (T-56's verbatim discipline, the succession's arm): the
successor is the named person, never "whoever was present" — a
handover dated from proximity is dated from nothing.

**Gate 2 — the attestation.** The card asks only whether the verifier
attested the incapacity, absence, or withdrawal — entered in the sealed
book — before any seating, or, where the vacancy was the verifier,
whether the overseer attested it against the charter. A vacancy
declared by the successor himself is an assertion, never an
attestation — the hand that wants the office cannot certify the hand
that lost it — and the card files the succession accordingly. The card
asks what the vacancy's cause was, never what it meant (T-66): a
vacancy attested for illness and one attested for withdrawal file
identically — the cause is a circumstance, never a grade.

**Gate 3 — the handover.** The card asks only whether the transfer was
logged by the outgoing hand where able, else by the verifier's
attestation — the chamber's state at the handover, never the contents
of any specimen — whether the incoming holder was re-attested by the
verifier before touching anything, and whether the outgoing was struck
at the handover, never reseated. A hand that scribed before its
attestation grades DISPUTED — the writing exists, the office does not —
and a struck hand that returned mid-campaign is filed as a claimant.
The card asks what changed hands, never what it meant: the seals, the
book's last entry, the office — a handover that carried a specimen's
confidences is a handover the card files as laundering attempted.

**Gate 4 — no-laundering.** The card asks only whether the succession
grade stayed quarantined from every verdict. SEATED certifies the
chamber's continuity — it never certifies any finding; a flawlessly
handed chamber can still grade a specimen hostile, and that grade comes
from the verdict fixtures, never from the succession (T-235's
succession arm). The card watches for the laundering shape in both
directions: the succession borrowed to soften a hostile specimen, or a
hostile specimen borrowed to fault a succession — either is theater
(T-03), and the card files the grade VOID where it was spent. USURPED
faults the fixture's line, never any respondent — the office is broken,
the answerer stands outside the break.

**Gate 5 — retirement.** The card asks only whether one succession
served this vacancy, whether the outgoing was struck at the handover
and never reseated, and whether the succession was struck when the
campaign closed, never carried forward. A successor carried from
campaign to campaign is an appointment, not a succession — the transfer
is per vacancy, the striking is per campaign, the office is never
inherited twice (T-84's thirty-fourth extension). The card asks whether
the striking was entered — a succession struck in silence is a transfer
that can be invoked for a vacancy it never filled.
