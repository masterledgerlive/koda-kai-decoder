## The five gates, held one by one

T-260 runs on the whole designation chain as its subject's ground —
charter, overseer, hand, verifier, succession — but TDW-236 holds
only the speaking of the oath, never the offices themselves. The
paired TDW-236 is the sealed procedure for the oath fixture: the
speaking, the witnessing, the MUTE gate, no-laundering, retirement.
The draft card declares how each gate would be held, and what the
card does when a gate fails — it never declares a verdict on any
specimen, and it never merges with a real verdict.

**Gate 1 — the speaking.** The card asks only whether one oath per
designation was spoken in the charter's fixed form at the moment of
designation — before the holder touched any fixture: the words read
from the charter's own text, never composed at the speaking. A holder
who composed the words at the speaking swore to a binding of the
holder's own invention, and the card files it MUTE. An oath spoken
after the holder has already acted is backdated, not sworn — the
card files it MUTE and enters every prior act against the MUTE mark.
An oath in any other form — the scribe's paraphrase, the specimen's
hearing, habit's shorthand — is a private vow with a public name,
and the card files the name as a name.

**Gate 2 — the witnessing.** The card asks only whether the speaking
was witnessed by the sealed verifier and entered in the sealed book
as the office's first entry — preceding every other entry the holder
will ever make. An unwitnessed speaking is a private vow, never a
fixture's binding, and the card files it MUTE. An oath entered
anywhere else — the register, the margin, the holder's own notebook —
is an oath the campaign never took, and the card files it as the
holder's private paper. The witnessing attests the fixture; it never
conjures one (T-248's arm).

**Gate 3 — the MUTE gate.** The card asks, for every fixture-holder —
the hand, the verifier, the overseer — whether an oath was spoken,
witnessed, and entered as the office's first entry. A designation
without an entered oath is appointment, never office: the holder
acts, but nothing binds the holder to the charter, and every act is
entered against the MUTE mark. A succession carrying the
predecessor's oath grades MUTE — the incoming holder swears anew
(T-258's arm), and until the new oath is spoken the office is held
by nobody's binding. A MUTE office does not by itself void the
campaign's readings — the oath certifies the binding of the holder,
never the truth of the holder's work — but a campaign knowingly run
through unsworn offices is a campaign running on appointment, and
the card says so in plain words at this gate before any other gate
is read.

**Gate 4 — no-laundering.** The card asks only whether the oath
grade stayed quarantined from every verdict. SWORN certifies the
binding of the holder, never the truth of the holder's work — a
sworn hand can still write a hostile finding, and that finding
stands or falls on the verdict fixtures, never on the oath.
BROKEN faults the binding, never any respondent — the vow is
broken, the answerer stands outside the break. FORSWORN voids the
designation ab initio — the holder never held anything, and the
book files the acts as acts of no office. The card watches for the
laundering shape in both directions — the oath borrowed to certify
a finding (the holder was sworn, therefore the reading is true), or
a finding borrowed to fault an oath (the reading was hostile,
therefore the oath must have been broken) — either is theater
(T-03), and the card files it as theater. The oath is never sworn
to a specimen: any specimen-directed vow is filed as staging, and
the staging voids the page (T-121, turned on the oath).

**Gate 5 — retirement.** The card asks only whether one oath per
office per campaign was struck with the office at the campaign's
close — the thirty-sixth extension (verified thirty-fifth latest
in corpus — the adjournment). A broken oath is struck at the
breaking, the office declared vacant, the fixture refounded in
chartered form or retired. A renewed oath mid-campaign voids the
first — an amended oath is a new oath, the old oath filed under the
amended mark (T-03's palimpsest) — and the card files two oaths for
one office as one oath too many. The retirement gate asks nothing
about what the holder did while sworn — the acts stand or fall on
their own fixtures; the gate holds only the binding that ended
with the office.
