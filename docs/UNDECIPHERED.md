# THE MOST WANTED — Unsolved Ciphers, Scripts & Codes

*The decoder's watch-list: everything humanity hasn't cracked yet, everything with
a bounty on it, everything biology already solved, and the recent cracks — each
with the human error that broke it. Grades: VERIFIED / CORROBORATED / CLAIMED /
SPECULATION. Designs are designs; unsolved stays unsolved until proven otherwise.*

## 1. Unsolved war documents & cryptographer-made ciphers

- **Pigeon NURP.40.TW.194** (1944) — 27 five-letter groups found in a chimney in
  Bletchingley, Surrey in 1982, still attached to a pigeon skeleton; believed
  D-Day-related. [VERIFIED existence, UNSOLVED content] GCHQ assessed it as
  one-time-pad-class — possibly unreadable forever. [CLAIMED — reported GCHQ
  assessment]
- **Tamam Shud / Somerton code** (1948) — scrap torn from the Rubaiyat of Omar
  Khayyam with five lines of letter groups, found with an unidentified man on a
  beach in Adelaide. [VERIFIED] The man was identified in 2022 as Carl Webb.
  [CORROBORATED — widely reported] The code remains unread.
- **McCormick notes** (1999) — Ricky McCormick's body found in Missouri with two
  notes of roughly thirty lines of cipher; the FBI published them in 2011 and
  asked the public for help. [VERIFIED] Still unsolved.
- **Shugborough inscription** (1748) — OUOSVAVV and D M carved on the Shepherd's
  Monument in Staffordshire. [VERIFIED] Unsolved for nearly three centuries.
- **D'Agapeyeff challenge cipher** (1939) — 196 digits printed as a "challenge" in
  the first edition of Alexander D'Agapeyeff's *Codes and Ciphers*; he later
  admitted he had forgotten the method, and it was dropped from later editions.
  [VERIFIED] Likely killed by its own author's hand — possibly a transcription
  error — rather than by any analyst. [CORROBORATED reading of the evidence]
- **Dorabella cipher** (1897) — Edward Elgar's enciphered note to Dora Penny
  (the 23-year-old stepdaughter of a family friend), slipped into the envelope
  with his wife Alice's thank-you letter of July 14, 1897, after the Elgars
  stayed at the Wolverhampton Rectory. 87 characters over 3 lines, ~24 distinct
  symbols built from one to three semicircles oriented in 8 directions; a small
  dot after the fifth character on the third line. [VERIFIED — Wikipedia] Penny
  never deciphered it; the note sat in a drawer forty years, surfaced in her
  1937 memoir *Edward Elgar: Memories of a Variation*, and the original is now
  lost. [VERIFIED] Two years later Elgar named Variation X of his *Enigma*
  Variations (Op. 36) "Dorabella" for her — the man who left the most famous
  unsolved musical cipher also wrote the most famous Enigma in classical music.
  [VERIFIED] Penny denied there was ever a shared private code between them —
  they wrote to each other in plain English — so the key died with Elgar alone.
  [CORROBORATED — thevintagenews / memoir reporting] Still unsolved as of
  October 2026: a 2026 self-published GitHub "DECODED" claim (OriginNeuralAI,
  "103 million evaluations," all-rights-reserved repo, no peer review) has no
  accepted reading and no independent verification — treated as CLAIMED until
  the field says otherwise [DOCTRINE]; and an independent long-running
  investigation (bugfishtm, updated ~Sep 2026) has ruled out a regular rotating
  key (p = 0.0003) and plain English substitution (z = −7.5 to −9.6) — the note
  is still unread. [CORROBORATED — the repo's own published results]
- **The decoder's lesson, in 87 symbols:** Dorabella is the Liber Primus
  problem in miniature — a custom alphabet with no crib, no bilingual, and a
  ciphertext too short for frequency to grip. Usage cribs can't touch it and
  statistics can't either. [DOCTRINE — information-theoretic]
- **Proper one-time-pad traffic** — mathematically unreadable by design.
  [VERIFIED — Shannon 1949] Venona was readable only because Soviet operators
  reused key pages. [VERIFIED] The pad is unbreakable; the *operators* are not.
- **Cicada 3301 / the Liber Primus** (2012–) — the internet's recruitment
  cipher, and the one the AI era hasn't cracked. First appeared January 2012
  on 4chan's /x/ board ("Hello. We are looking for highly intelligent
  individuals"), PGP-signed; three annual puzzle rounds (2012, 2013, 2014)
  chaining steganography (OutGuess), RSA, book ciphers, Tor hidden services,
  payphones, GPS coordinates, QR codes. The 2012 and 2013 chains are fully
  documented end-to-end — solved, archived, reproducible. [CORROBORATED —
  Rolling Stone and the solver-community archives]
- The 2014 round led into the **Liber Primus**: ~75 pages in a 29-rune script
  (Gematria Primus), each rune carrying both a letter-cluster and a prime
  number. Roughly 17–19 pages were solved 2014–2017 — and *every* solved page
  fell to keys guessable from the puzzle's own vocabulary (DIVINITY,
  CIRCUMFERENCE), Atbash, Vigenère, or totient/prime keystreams. Usage cribs
  again: the Enigma lesson repeated, this time by the puzzle's own authors.
  [CORROBORATED — solver-community records; connortumbleson 2024]
- The remaining ~55 pages are unsolved as of October 2026. The 2026 state of
  the art is honest about why: flat rune frequencies, a doublet deficit
  (86 observed vs ~447 expected across ~13,000 runes, z = −17), every
  structured-key family excluded — statistically indistinguishable from a
  one-time pad with one deliberate constraint (no repeated runes).
  Ciphertext alone cannot solve it; the key was never published. Documented
  2024–2026 AI/agent attempts produced no breakthrough. [CORROBORATED —
  wulfic 2026-09-29 state-of-art review; dukotah 2026 campaign findings]
- Last authenticated 3301 activity: an OpenPGP-signed "Beware false paths"
  message, April 2017. No verified identity behind Cicada 3301 has ever been
  established. [CORROBORATED]
- **The decoder's lesson, in reverse:** the same AI-agent era that cracked the
  217-year-old Napoleon briefing and the 1941 MVUEH Enigma message (both §5,
  2026) hit a wall here — because those ciphers leaked usage, and these pages
  leak nothing. Solved pages were broken by usage flaws (thematic keys);
  unsolved pages behave as a pad. No key, no break. [DOCTRINE — Shannon 1949,
  information-theoretic]
- **Rohonc Codex** (Hungary/Transylvania, ~16th century) — 448-page illustrated
  manuscript written in an unknown script (~792 distinct symbols recorded,
  right-to-left layout, 80+ illustrations), from the Batthyány family library
  at Rohonc (now Rechnitz, Austria), donated to the Hungarian Academy of
  Sciences in 1838. No accepted full reading. [VERIFIED manuscript facts]
- 2018: Levente Király and Gábor Tokai proposed a partial decipherment via an
  Old-Hungarian-script variant, early Hungarian dialect. [CLAIMED — no
  accepted reading]
- **2026 breakthrough (peer-reviewed):** Bobory, Láng, Király & Tokai, "New
  evidence on the Rohonc Codex: authorship, provenance, and script continuity"
  (*Cryptologia*, 2026) — a recently discovered contemporary letter with four
  previously unknown pages written in the Rohonc script. Key claims: the codex
  entered the Batthyány library around 1571; produced in Hungary or Central
  Europe; the author was an erudite scribe (deák); the 19th-century
  forgery/hoax hypothesis is definitively ruled out — the script survived in
  at least two independent contexts, one a dated 16th-century source; and the
  four new pages read coherently under the same decryption method previously
  applied to the codex — the first independent replication of the 2018
  decipherment. Open questions remain. [CORROBORATED — peer-reviewed paper]
- A 2025–26 self-published GitHub project claims a "complete" decipherment at
  "99.2% confidence" (self-graded, no peer review). [SPECULATION — judge a
  claim by who else verified it, never by who wrote the confidence table]

## 3. Ancient tablets & scripts

- **Indus script** (~2600–1900 BCE) — roughly four thousand inscriptions, most
  under thirty symbols, no bilingual text, no consensus on the underlying
  language. [VERIFIED unsolved] New claimant, Oct 2026: Bharath Rao
  ("Yajnadevam") circulates "A Cryptanalytic Decipherment of the Indus Script"
  (99 pages) — sequential regex + set-intersection sign resolution claimed to
  meet Shannon's unicity distance, yielding a proto-abugida read as post-Vedic
  Sanskrit with sign-to-Brahmi continuity. Not peer-reviewed; scholarly
  consensus remains "no accepted decipherment." [CLAIMED — popular-archaeology.com
  interview; author says journal submission pending polish] Worth watching:
  the claim ships with an explicit falsifiability criterion — if it fails,
  we learn how cryptanalytic rigor fails on this corpus.
  Counterweight (~Aug 2026): "On the Non-Specificity of Statistical Measures
  Used in Script Decipherment" (arXiv 2608.02999v1) — SIGIL, a deterministic
  generative system, produces seal-like inscriptions for a fictional
  administrative world where every text has meaning (dedication, office,
  entitlement, administrative event, with the semantic derivation stored
  beside it) but no text has linguistic encoding. A constructive
  counterexample to reading conditional entropy, block entropy,
  rank-frequency laws, positional restrictions, predictability, network
  structure, and classifier output as signs of language — administrative and
  emblematic practice generates the same statistical signatures. Directly
  engages dictionary/pattern-matching proposals like Yajnadevam's above:
  statistics cannot diagnose language; a decipherment must be self-proving.
  [CLAIMED — preprint; abstract/results via search; primary not read]
  New claimant, May 2026 preprint (logged Oct 2026): Tristen Kyle Pierson
  (Glossa-Lab / BitConcepts LLC), "A Complete Computational Decipherment
  Hypothesis for the Indus Script" (Preprint v3, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20401711,
  not peer-reviewed) — assigns Proto-Dravidian readings to all 605 known
  signs: 413 HIGH-confidence under a five-layer evidence hierarchy (two or
  more independent sources per sign), 192 allograph-inferred and treated
  provisional. Method: positional analysis, bigram/trigram collocational
  methods, DEDR rebus matching, simulated annealing against a 7,514-word
  Dravidian language model, across two independent corpora (~10,327
  inscriptions, 76+ sites). Headline statistics (unverified): SA 83.7%
  consistency; tripartite INITIAL-MEDIAL-TERMINAL grammar 6.3x above null
  (p<0.001); conditional entropy 5.384 bits vs the 3.5-bit metrological
  maximum (falsifies the Farmer-Sproat-Witzel non-linguistic hypothesis).
  Directly engages the record above: falsifies Yajnadevam 2024's Sanskrit
  hypothesis at 0/34 agreement. Ships explicit falsifiability criteria and
  open code/anchor tables — a claimant built to be disprovable, the kind this
  list exists to file. Scoreboard unchanged: no peer review, no independent
  validation, no consensus — Indus remains unsolved, the $1M prize open.
  [CLAIMED — preprint v3, company-authored, not peer-reviewed;
  abstract/methods/results read directly via the GitHub-hosted manuscript]
  New instrument, May 2026 (logged Oct 21, 2026): Boris Kriger & T. A. Hunt,
  "Positional constraints, sequence uniqueness, and stroke numerals in Indus
  seal inscriptions from Mohenjo-Daro: a statistical analysis" (IIIR
  Computational Humanities and Cultural Systems, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19103880)
  — AI-assisted statistical analysis of the Mohenjo-Daro seal corpus:
  positional constraints, sequence-uniqueness patterns, stroke numerals.
  The run surfaced one anomalous sign classification (P120) the human

- **Byblos pseudo-hieroglyphic script** (~1800–1500 BCE, Byblos, Lebanon) —

  Sign-count lands squarely in the syllabary band — too many for a pure
  alphabet, too few for a logography — but no bilingual text exists, the corpus

  produced. It sits in the same \"big five\" as Linear A, the Indus script,
  Rongorongo, and Cretan hieroglyphs. [VERIFIED unsolved; sign-count and
  discovery facts CORROBORATED — standard epigraphic literature; Dunand's own

- **Voynich manuscript** (Beinecke MS 408, parchment 1404–1438) — two new items,
  logged Oct 15, 2026. (a) Two researchers in Ottawa (February 2026) read the
  script as angular Glagolitic cursive — a medieval Croatian script tradition
  never previously considered by Voynich researchers — working from scribe
  handwriting habits rather than letter shapes; the gallows characters become
  standard medieval abbreviation marks for consonant clusters. The resulting key
  produces Croatian text at about ninety-five percent morphological coverage
  across nearly thirty-eight thousand tokens, with native-speaker validation,
  and the contents resolve to pharmacy recipes consistent with medieval
  Dubrovnik apothecaries. All data, code, and methods are published for
  reproduction. The second herbal-pharmacy reading to enter the catalogue this
  cycle. One claimant team, no peer review, no independent replication yet —
  claimed until a second lab reproduces the key on held-out folios. (b) A
  researcher published an exhaustive geometric and compositional structural
  analysis (September 2026, open preprints) — over eighty-three thousand
  register configurations and half a million pair-order tests — with a full
  reproducibility package, while explicitly claiming no decipherment and
  carefully separating observed findings from speculative ones. The honest
  structuralist lane: a falsifiable hypothesis with published nulls.
  (c) In twenty twenty four, paleographer Lisa Fagin Davis examined Yale's
  multispectral images and found three faded columns in the right margin of
  the first page: a standard alphabet, a column of Voynich characters, and a
  second alphabet shifted by one letter — a seventeenth-century decryption
  attempt in the hand of Johannes Marcus Marci, the Prague physician who
  owned the book in the sixteen sixties. It does not work. The only
  known-name decryption attempt on the manuscript on record, and a
  documented failure: a seventeenth-century owner ran his key against the
  text and got nothing, which constrains the easy-substitution hypothesis
  more than another modern claim does. Corroborated by Davis's own write-up
  and independent coverage, with the images public since twenty twenty four.
  (d) A self-published site now claims ninety-eight percent confidence on a
  Latin reading, graded by its own internal numbers with no outside
  verification — rejected on intake as speculation. (e) "The Composite-Glyph
  Protocol Hypothesis: A Multimodal Structural Framework for the Voynich
  Manuscript" (Zenodo, June 2026, first version; new to record) — a
  provisional structural framework that is explicitly not a decipherment:
  the manuscript's script read as a composite-glyph protocol linking glyph
  structure, imagery, and page function across the plant, astronomical,
  bathing, pharmaceutical, and recipe sections, with a code correspondence
  table, a page-type switching model, scoring functions, annotation
  templates, null-model comparisons, and falsification criteria. It joins
  the honest structuralist lane — a research program for testing whether
  glyph structure, imagery, and layout jointly constrain meaning, rather
  than another claim of a key. CLAIMED — a preprint; the findings belong to
  its authors. (f) A categorical naive Bayes model of the manuscript's
  morphological slot dynamics (preprint, October sixth, twenty twenty-six;
  new to record) — an independent researcher's structural reframing built
  with an AI assistant: the text decomposed into sixteen morphological slots
  under strict sound-pattern constraints, classified with a Laplace-smoothed
  naive Bayes model. The empirical test page splits roughly fifty-one
  percent pharmaceutical against forty-nine percent balneological while
  rejecting botanical and astronomical domains at zero. The paper's headline
  — that this mathematically rules out a medieval hoax — outruns its
  evidence: it demonstrates a coherent, domain-adaptive generative grammar,
  not an authorship verdict. No peer review, no replication yet — claimed.
  (g) "The Voynich Manuscript as a Writing System" (open preprint, October
  twenty twenty-six; new to record) — the falsification battery the field's
  honesty standard pointed at: homophonic-substitution solvers that recover
  sixty-nine to ninety-eight percent of real words in known texts across
  fourteen languages manage at most thirty-six percent on the Voynich; a
  simulated-annealing solver calibrated at ninety-eight to one hundred
  percent key recovery on one hundred and twenty-seven positive controls
  scores at chance on the Voynich in seventy-one languages, even reading
  backwards; a previously published key fails outside the pages it was
  derived from. Explicitly not a decipherment — claimed — but now the
  reference example for the intake bar: calibrated controls, published
  negatives, no self-grading.
  (h) Colin Layfield (University of Malta) and Lisa Fagin Davis, "Singulion
  Structure and the Voynich Manuscript" (Digital Medievalist, peer-reviewed,
  October twenty twenty-six; new to record — method and source texture for
  the on-record mis-binding finding): a numerical-analysis tool comparing
  blocks of text found attached pages scoring highly for coherence even
  across many intervening pages — the folded sheets were likely stacked as
  loose "singulions" (like Christmas cards) and mis-bound in the standard
  nested arrangement in the mid-to-late fourteen hundreds, so the book has
  been read in the wrong page order for centuries. The visual evidence
  agrees: illustrations of streams or pipes that run into the gutter and
  fail to match across the spread join up once the pages are treated as a
  loose stack. The only other medieval manuscripts known in a singulion
  arrangement are north African Qurans likely handed out as small
  instructional booklets — a pedagogical parallel, not a cryptographic one.
  Paleographical analysis points to up to five scribes; the lone-genius
  authorship model is out. Davis's further read — a book of women's folk
  knowledge preserved community to community — is flagged by the authors as
  unproven, the honest null inside the breakthrough. Corroborated (The
  Guardian, October 6, twenty twenty-six, reporting the peer-reviewed
  article; the article itself was not read first-party this pass).

## 4. Biological codes

- **The genetic code** — cracked 1961–66: Nirenberg and Matthaei's poly-U
  experiment (1961), then Holley and Khorana; all 64 codons mapped by 1966.

- **Human Genome Project** (completed 2003) — the book was read. [VERIFIED]
  Understanding what it says is the live frontier. [SPECULATION where specific

- **The histone code** (proposed 2000, still uncracked) — Strahl & Allis's
  hypothesis: combinations of post-translational modifications on histone tails
  (methylation, acetylation, phosphorylation, ubiquitination) are read by
  effector proteins as a combinatorial code regulating gene expression.
  [VERIFIED — the published hypothesis, Cell 2000] Twenty-six years on, no
  codebook exists: crosstalk between marks, context dependence, and cell-type
  specificity mean the field has an alphabet but no grammar. [CORROBORATED —
  review literature] It is the genetic code's unsolved sibling — same family,
  no Rosetta stone — and unlike Nirenberg's poly-U there is no single experiment

  framing] The decoder's angle: sequence-to-function deep models (the Enformer

  pre-Ventris position: the machine can translate but can't show its work.
  [CLAIMED — capabilities vendor-published; independent benchmarks partial]

## 5. Recently cracked — and the human error that broke each

- **Zodiac 340** (2020) — cracked by Oranchak, Oranchak, and van Eycke; the
  killer's own mistakes (repeated sections, transposition errors) became the
  cribs. [VERIFIED]
- **Copiale cipher** (2011) — homophonic 18th-century German manuscript; the
  mixed system (abstract symbols plus Roman letters) was the tell. [VERIFIED]
- **Borg cipher** (2016) — 17th-century, closed with computational methods.
  [VERIFIED]
- **Napoleon 1809** (2026) — homophonic military briefing, ~1,300 cipher units,
  155 symbols, unread for 217 years; cracked by GPT-6 Astra in about six hours
  from a single scanned image, reviewed and marked solved by Satoshi Tomokiyo
  (Cryptiana). The decoded troop dispositions re-dated the letter from 1807 to
  late March 1809 — the archive's own date was the human error. [CORROBORATED]
- **MVUEH Enigma** (2026) — an autonomous agent was told to "break any unsolved
  message," chose the unsolved July 1941 signal, built its own Enigma simulator
  and software Bombe, and broke it in about two days; validated by Frode
  Weierud. The obstacles were operator typos, a wrong wheel order, and dirty
  transcription — usage flaws, not the machine. [CORROBORATED]
- **Mary Stuart's 57 letters** (2023) — ciphered letters of Mary, Queen of
  Scots from her English captivity (1578–1584), decoded by Lasry, Biermann,
  and Tomokiyo and published in *Cryptologia*. The 57 letters sat mislabeled
  in the Bibliothèque nationale de France's digital archive as early-16th-
  century Italian papers; their royal provenance was unrecognized until after
  the cipher fell. [VERIFIED] The cipher was a 219-symbol nomenclator with
  homophones and diacritic modifiers — including a "delete previous" symbol.
  The human error was the catalog label, not the mathematics: seven plaintext
  copies of the same letters in the British Archives confirmed the reading.
  Doctrine hold: the Gauntlet interrogates provenance labels before content.
  [DOCTRINE]
- **Dhofari 1** (2026) — pre-Islamic script of Oman's Dhofar region, undeciphered
  since the early 20th century; cracked by Ahmad Al-Jallad (Ohio State),
  published in *Jaarbericht Ex Oriente Lux*. The break: three inscriptions
  were not words but the Dhofari alphabet — abecedaries painted on cave walls
  and carved into wadi stones. The corpus carried its own key table; the
  human error was the premise, not the cipher. [CORROBORATED]
- **The pattern:** every break in this section leaned on somebody's mistake — a
  typo, a reused key, a misfiled date, a crib left in the habits. The doctrine's
  "usage flaws first" ordering holds again. [DOCTRINE]
- **Oct 2026 discipline pair (from this track's pass):** (a) serious independent
  cryptanalysis now ships reproducibility suites as standard practice — the bar
  any claim must clear on intake; (b) a claim that grades itself (anonymous
  Voynich "DECODED") is rejected on intake — SPECULATION until an external judge
  grades it. The Gauntlet judges a claim by who else verified it, never by who
  wrote the confidence table. [DOCTRINE]

## 7. Evaluation layer — machine-readable corpora

*The watch-list's machine-graded twin: standardized corpora that let automated decipherment claims be scored rather than asserted. A benchmark is not a solution; Track-D results are hypotheses, never verdicts.*

- **cipher_benchmark (matthewdgreen/cipher_benchmark).** A standardized benchmark dataset for evaluating AI-assisted classical cipher analysis tools (public project, created April 2026; currently private while image redistribution rights are resolved). Nine hundred and five solved/calibration records — Copiale, Borg, DECODE/Gallica manuscripts, synthetic multilingual substitution sets, Zodiac parity records, Kryptos sections one through three, the Feynman challenge ciphers, Poe's Gold Bug, plus a neutral probe — alongside a parallel two-hundred-sixty-two-record unsolved area covering the famous open ciphers: the Voynich Manuscript (227 records, with canonical folio transliterations produced under direct permission), unsolved Zodiac variants, the Scorpion ciphers, Kryptos K4, Beale 1 & 3, D'Agapeyeff, Dorabella, the Somerton code, Ricky McCormick's notes, the Lawrence Public Library cryptogram, the Blitz ciphers, and more; the Rohonc Codex and a DECODE-undecrypted subset are planned. Four tracks: image-to-transcription, transcription-to-plaintext, image-to-plaintext, and — for the unsolved area — Track D (image-to-hypothesis) with a reproducible review rubric, since there is no ground truth to score against. Every record carries a rights class. Significance: the Most Wanted list now has a scored twin — decipherment claims get a reproducible evaluation model, and Track D is the honest shape for hypotheses against ciphers with no known answer.
